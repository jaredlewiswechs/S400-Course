# Assessment Guide — M200 Geometry of the Built World

## Assessment Philosophy

Assessment in M200 measures whether students can **apply geometric reasoning to real-world spatial problems**. Procedural skills (computing area, writing proofs) matter, but they are always connected to purpose — designing, building, measuring, and analyzing the physical world. Every unit project requires students to apply geometry to architecture, engineering, or spatial analysis.

## Grading Structure

| Component | Weight | Description |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Construction & Measurement Labs | 30% | GeoGebra constructions, physical models, RStudio spatial labs |
| Proofs & Problem Sets | 25% | Formal and informal geometric proofs, application problems |

| Participation & Checkpoints | 15% | Warm-ups, peer review, checkpoint quizzes |

## Construction & Measurement Lab Rubric

Used for GeoGebra, physical construction, and RStudio spatial labs.

| Criterion | 5 (Excellent) | 4 (Proficient) | 3 (Developing) | 2 (Beginning) | 1 (Incomplete) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Accuracy** | Construction/calculation is mathematically correct | Mostly correct with minor measurement errors | Correct approach but errors affecting result | Significant errors | No meaningful work |
| **Precision** | Measurements are precise; constructions are clean and well-labeled | Minor imprecision that doesn't affect conclusions | Some imprecision affecting reliability | Substantially imprecise | No measurement |
| **Justification** | Explains WHY the construction/calculation works using geometric vocabulary and reasoning | Provides justification with minor gaps | States a reason but lacks precision | Attempt at justification but incorrect | No justification |
| **Technology Use** | Effectively uses the appropriate tool (GeoGebra, R, Desmos) to verify and visualize | Mostly effective tool use | Tool used but not for verification | Minimal tool engagement | No tool use |

**Lab Grading Scale:** Each criterion is scored 1–5. Total = 20 points per standard lab. Extended labs may count as 1.5x.

## Proof Rubric

Used for two-column and paragraph proofs.

| Criterion | Full Credit | Partial Credit | Minimal Credit |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Logical completeness** | Every step follows logically; no gaps in reasoning | Minor gaps that don't undermine the conclusion | Significant gaps; conclusion not fully supported |
| **Correct reasons** | Every statement is paired with a valid reason (definition, postulate, or theorem) | Most reasons are correct; 1–2 minor errors | Several incorrect or missing reasons |
| **Notation and format** | Proper geometric notation; clear two-column or paragraph format | Minor notation errors | Significant notation or format issues |
| **Given/Prove** | Given and Prove statements are correctly stated | Minor issues with Given/Prove | Given/Prove missing or incorrect |

## Checkpoint Quiz Format

Each unit includes one 25-minute checkpoint quiz. Format:

| Section | Questions | Points | Description |
|---------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Multiple Choice | 5 | 10 | Concept identification and vocabulary |
| Short Computation | 3 | 12 | Find angles, sides, areas, or apply formulas |
| Proof or Application | 1 | 8 | Short proof (fill-in or write) or applied problem |
| **Total** | **9** | **30** | |

Quizzes are individual, closed-note. Calculators permitted. Formula sheets NOT provided (students should know or derive formulas).

## Project Rubric (Standard)

Used for all unit projects.

| Criterion | Excellent (5) | Proficient (4) | Developing (3) | Beginning (2) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Mathematical Accuracy** | All geometric calculations and proofs are correct | Minor errors that don't undermine the project | Some errors that weaken the analysis | Significant mathematical errors |
| **Real-World Connection** | Architecture/engineering context is meaningful and well-integrated | Context present but connection is surface-level | Context mentioned but geometry feels disconnected | No real-world connection |
| **Visualization** | Diagrams, models, or digital constructions are clear, accurate, and well-labeled | Mostly clear with minor issues | Present but not well-integrated | Missing or irrelevant |
| **Communication** | Written explanation uses geometric vocabulary precisely and interprets results | Mostly clear with minor vocabulary gaps | Some explanation but imprecise | Little to no written explanation |
| **Completeness** | All required components present and thorough | Most components present | Some components missing | Major components missing |

## Capstone Design Project Rubric (Unit 6)

| Criterion | Excellent (A) | Proficient (B) | Developing (C) | Beginning (D) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Floor Plan & Design** (10 pts) | Clear, dimensioned floor plan with required room types | Mostly clear with minor omissions | Plan present but dimensions missing or unclear | No floor plan or severely incomplete |
| **Area Calculations** (10 pts) | All room areas and total floor area correctly computed | Minor calculation errors | Some areas computed, others missing | Few or no area calculations |
| **Volume Calculations** (10 pts) | Interior volume and 3D feature volumes correct | Minor errors | Partial volume calculations | Missing |
| **Circle Geometry** (10 pts) | Circular element included with correct arc/sector/circumference calculations | Minor errors in circular calculations | Circular element present but calculations weak | No circular element |
| **Proof** (10 pts) | Relevant coordinate or congruence proof, correctly written | Proof attempted with minor errors | Proof attempted but incomplete | No proof |
| **Visualizations** (10 pts) | Floor plan + 3D model or physical model, both well-labeled | One strong visualization | Visualization present but unclear | No visualization |
| **Written Analysis** (5 pts) | Clear, uses geometric vocabulary, connects geometry to design decisions | Adequate analysis | Brief or vague | Missing |

## Accommodations and Differentiation

| Student Need | Accommodation |
|-------------|---------------|
| Students who struggle with proofs | Provide proof templates with sentence starters; allow paragraph proofs instead of two-column |
| Advanced students | Extend with non-Euclidean geometry preview, additional trig applications, or complex 3D design |
| ELL students | Provide bilingual vocabulary lists (English/Spanish); allow diagrams with minimal text |
| Students with limited technology access | Provide printed GeoGebra screenshots; allow hand-drawn constructions for all labs |
| Students with fine motor challenges | Allow GeoGebra for all constructions (instead of physical compass/straightedge) |
| Students with test anxiety | Offer checkpoint quizzes as take-home or allow a second attempt |
| Students entering without S500 head start | Provide a 1-page orientation to `sf` package basics before the coordinate geometry unit |
