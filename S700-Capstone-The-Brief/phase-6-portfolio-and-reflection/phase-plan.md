# Phase 6 — Portfolio & Reflection

## Phase Overview

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Phase | 6 of 6 |
| Title | Portfolio & Reflection |
| Duration | 30 hours (~3.5 weeks) |
| Driving Question | *What have you built, what have you learned, and what will you do next?* |
| Key Deliverables | (1) Portfolio website or compiled document; (2) Reflective essay; (3) Brief revisions based on defense feedback |

## Phase Narrative

The defense is over. Now students step back and see the full arc of their work — not just S700, but the entire S400–S700 sequence. Phase 6 has three purposes. First, students revise their policy brief based on panel feedback, producing a final "post-defense" version that incorporates the panel's strongest critiques. Second, students compile a portfolio of their best work from all four courses — a tangible record of growth from "Your Money as Data" through the policy brief. Third, students write a reflective essay on their intellectual journey: what they learned about data, about social science, about themselves as researchers, and about what it means to be an informed citizen. This phase also includes a public showcase (if feasible) and course evaluation.

## Learning Objectives

- Revise substantively based on expert feedback (defense panel)
- Compile a professional portfolio demonstrating growth across four courses
- Reflect critically on learning, methodology, and civic engagement
- Present work to a broader audience (showcase)

## Lesson Sequence

### Week 1: Post-Defense Revision (≈8.3 hours)

#### Lesson 6.1 — Defense Debrief (2 hrs)
**Activity:**
- Students review their defense performance:
  - What questions did the panel ask?
  - Which questions were hardest? Why?
  - What feedback did the panel give?
  - What would you change about your brief based on the defense?
- Students create a "Revision Plan" listing 3–5 specific changes they will make to the brief.

#### Lesson 6.2 — Post-Defense Brief Revision (4 hrs)
**Activity:**
- Students revise their policy brief based on defense feedback and their revision plan.
- Common post-defense revisions:
  - Strengthening the discussion of limitations (the panel almost always pushes on this).
  - Adding a robustness check that the panel suggested.
  - Clarifying a statistical explanation that was confusing during Q&A.
  - Sharpening the recommendations to be more specific.
  - Adding a counterargument the panel raised.
- The revised brief is the FINAL version — the one that goes in the portfolio.
- Commit to GitHub with tag: `git tag v2.0-post-defense`.

#### Lesson 6.3 — Peer Feedback on Revisions (2.3 hrs)
**Activity:**
- Students exchange post-defense drafts with a partner who attended their defense.
- Partner checks: Did the student address the panel's main concerns?
- Quick read-through and 1-paragraph feedback.
- Final knit and submission.

---

### Week 2: Portfolio Assembly (≈8.3 hours)

#### Lesson 6.4 — The Portfolio: What to Include (2 hrs)
**Activity:**
- The portfolio is a curated collection of the student's best work across all four courses. It demonstrates growth from beginner to original researcher.
- Required contents:

| Course | Required Item | Description |
|--------|---------------|-------------|
| S400 | Budget Dashboard OR Price Investigation | Early R work; first visualizations |
| S400 | Capstone: Economic Argument (excerpt) | First full analytical product |
| S500 | Redistricting Analysis OR Representative Report Card | Mapping and political data analysis |
| S500 | Capstone: Accountability Report (excerpt) | Government data analysis |
| S600 | Country Brief OR Trade Network Analysis | Global data, inferential statistics |
| S600 | One R Lab with Inference (best example) | Demonstrates statistical rigor |
| S700 | Final Policy Brief (complete) | The capstone deliverable |
| S700 | Defense slides | The oral presentation |
| All | Reflective Essay (Phase 6) | Personal and intellectual reflection |

- Format: Students compile as either:
  - A GitHub Pages website (for students comfortable with web tools).
  - An R Markdown "portfolio document" that renders to HTML/PDF.
  - A Google Sites page with embedded R outputs.

#### Lesson 6.5 — Curating and Formatting (3.5 hrs)
**Activity:**
- Students retrieve their best work from prior courses (from personal files or course folders).
- For each item: Write a 2–3 sentence "portfolio note" explaining:
  - What the assignment was.
  - What R/statistics skills it demonstrates.
  - What you would do differently now.
- Format all items consistently. Add a table of contents or navigation.
- The portfolio should tell a story of growth — from basic bar charts to original research.

#### Lesson 6.6 — Portfolio Workshop (2.8 hrs)
**Activity:**
- Peer review of portfolios:
  - Is the progression visible? Can you see the student's growth?
  - Are the portfolio notes helpful and specific?
  - Is the formatting clean and professional?
- Students refine based on feedback.

---

### Week 3: Reflection and Celebration (≈8.3 hours)

#### Lesson 6.7 — Writing the Reflective Essay (3 hrs)
**Activity:**
- Students write a 1,000–1,500 word reflective essay. Prompts (students may choose or combine):
  1. **The journey:** "When you started S400, you analyzed your personal budget. Now you've defended an original policy brief before a panel. What changed? What skills did you develop? What was the hardest part?"
  2. **The tools:** "R was introduced as 'the calculator.' How do you think about it now? How has learning to code changed how you approach questions? Is there a moment when R went from frustrating to powerful?"
  3. **The data:** "You've worked with FRED, BLS, Census, World Bank, WHO, UCDP, ProPublica, and more. What surprised you about government and global data? What did you learn about what data can and cannot tell you?"
  4. **The citizen:** "This curriculum was designed to make you a data-literate citizen. Do you feel like one? When you encounter a political claim or policy debate, do you think about it differently than you did before?"
  5. **The researcher:** "Your S700 project was original research. What did you learn about the research process that you didn't expect? What would you do differently if you started over?"
- Writing quality expectations: Honest, specific, reflective. Cite specific assignments, moments, or skills. Avoid generic platitudes.

#### Lesson 6.8 — Public Showcase (3 hrs)
**Activity:**
- If feasible, the school hosts a public showcase of student work.
- Format options:
  - **Poster session:** Students print their brief's executive summary and key visualizations as a poster. Guests circulate and ask questions.
  - **Presentation evening:** Selected students present their briefs to families and community members.
  - **Digital showcase:** A class website with links to each student's portfolio.
- Audience: Families, other students, teachers, administrators, community members, school board members.
- This is a celebration of accomplishment. Four courses. Two to three years of cumulative skill building. Original research defended before a panel. These students are data-literate citizens.

#### Lesson 6.9 — Course and Sequence Evaluation (1 hr)
**Activity:**
- Anonymous student evaluations:
  - What worked best in S700? In the full sequence?
  - What would you change?
  - What was the most valuable skill you developed?
  - What advice would you give to next year's S400 students?
- Brief class discussion: Highlights, memories, and "what I wish I'd known."

#### Lesson 6.10 — Final Submission and Celebration (1 hrs)
**Activity:**
- Final portfolio and reflective essay submitted.
- All GitHub repositories cleaned up and tagged with `final`.
- Teacher distributes certificates of completion (if school supports this).
- Closing message: "You entered this sequence not knowing what a scatterplot was. You are leaving having conducted original research, mastered statistical inference, and produced a professional policy brief. Whatever you do next — college, career, civic life — you have the tools to ask hard questions and demand data-driven answers."

## Reflective Essay Rubric (35 points)

| Criterion | Excellent (A) | Proficient (B) | Developing (C) | Beginning (D) | Points |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|--------|
| **Specificity** | Cites specific assignments, tools, moments of learning; uses concrete examples | Some specific references; mostly general | Mostly general; few specifics | Entirely generic | 10 |
| **Depth** | Demonstrates genuine insight about learning, research, or citizenship; shows changed thinking | Shows thoughtful reflection with some depth | Surface-level reflection | No evidence of reflection | 10 |
| **Growth Narrative** | Clear arc from beginning to end; reader can see how the student changed | Some growth described | Minimal growth narrative | No growth evident | 10 |
| **Writing Quality** | Clear, personal, well-organized; appropriate tone | Mostly clear | Somewhat unclear or unfocused | Poor quality | 5 |
