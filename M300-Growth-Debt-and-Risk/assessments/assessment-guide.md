# Assessment Guide — M300 Growth, Debt, and Risk

## Assessment Philosophy

Assessment in M300 measures whether students can **apply mathematical reasoning to real-world financial, probabilistic, and structural problems**. Both paths emphasize application over abstraction — procedural skills matter, but they are always connected to purpose. Path A students demonstrate mastery through financial analysis, risk assessment, and data-driven recommendations. Path B students demonstrate mastery through algebraic modeling, system analysis, and prediction. Every unit project requires students to apply mathematics to decisions, systems, or datasets that exist outside the classroom.

## Spreadsheet & R Lab Rubric (Path A)

Used for financial modeling, simulation, and data analysis labs.

| Criterion | 5 (Excellent) | 4 (Proficient) | 3 (Developing) | 2 (Beginning) | 1 (Incomplete) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Accuracy** | All calculations and formulas are mathematically correct | Mostly correct with minor computational errors | Correct approach but errors affecting results | Significant errors in formulas or calculations | No meaningful work |
| **Model Design** | Spreadsheet or R script is well-organized, labeled, and uses appropriate formulas/functions | Mostly organized with minor labeling gaps | Functional but poorly organized | Disorganized or hard to follow | No model |
| **Interpretation** | Results are correctly interpreted in the real-world context with precise financial/statistical vocabulary | Interpretation present with minor gaps | States results but lacks contextual connection | Attempt at interpretation but incorrect | No interpretation |
| **Technology Use** | Effectively uses spreadsheet functions, R commands, or Desmos to verify and visualize | Mostly effective tool use | Tool used but not for verification or visualization | Minimal tool engagement | No tool use |

**Lab Grading Scale:** Each criterion is scored 1–5. Total = 20 points per standard lab. Extended labs may count as 1.5x.

## Data & Modeling Lab Rubric (Path B)

Used for RStudio, Desmos, and graphing calculator labs.

| Criterion | 5 (Excellent) | 4 (Proficient) | 3 (Developing) | 2 (Beginning) | 1 (Incomplete) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Accuracy** | All algebraic work and computations are correct | Mostly correct with minor errors | Correct approach but errors affecting the result | Significant algebraic errors | No meaningful work |
| **Modeling** | Appropriate function type selected; model fits data well with justification | Model selected with minor justification gaps | Model attempted but poor fit or weak justification | Inappropriate model or no justification | No model |
| **Visualization** | Graphs are clear, well-labeled, and effectively support the analysis | Mostly clear with minor issues | Graphs present but not well-integrated | Minimal or unclear graphs | No visualization |
| **Technology Use** | Effectively uses R, Desmos, or calculator to explore, verify, and present | Mostly effective tool use | Tool used but superficially | Minimal tool engagement | No tool use |

**Lab Grading Scale:** Each criterion is scored 1–5. Total = 20 points per standard lab. Extended labs may count as 1.5x.

## Checkpoint Quiz Format

Each unit includes one 25-minute checkpoint quiz. Format:

| Section | Questions | Points | Description |
|---------|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Multiple Choice | 5 | 10 | Concept identification and vocabulary |
| Short Computation | 3 | 12 | Apply formulas, solve equations, or compute values |
| Application Problem | 1 | 8 | Multi-step real-world problem requiring setup and solution |
| **Total** | **9** | **30** | |

Quizzes are individual, closed-note. Calculators permitted (financial or graphing, depending on path). Formula sheets NOT provided — students should know or derive key formulas.

## Project Rubric (Standard — Both Paths)

Used for all unit projects.

| Criterion | Excellent (5) | Proficient (4) | Developing (3) | Beginning (2) |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Mathematical Accuracy** | All calculations, formulas, and algebraic work are correct | Minor errors that don't undermine the analysis | Some errors that weaken the conclusions | Significant mathematical errors |

| **Communication** | Written explanation uses precise mathematical/financial vocabulary and interprets all results | Mostly clear with minor vocabulary gaps | Some explanation but imprecise language | Little to no written explanation |
| **Completeness** | All required components present and thorough | Most components present | Some components missing | Major components missing |

## Accommodations and Differentiation

| Student Need | Accommodation |
|-------------|---------------|
| Students who struggle with financial concepts (Path A) | Provide vocabulary glossaries with real-world examples; allow collaborative work on initial labs |
| Students who struggle with algebraic manipulation (Path B) | Provide worked examples library; allow Desmos/calculator verification alongside hand computation |
| Advanced students (Path A) | Extend with investment portfolio analysis, actuarial science preview, or advanced regression techniques |
| Advanced students (Path B) | Extend with partial fractions, conic sections, or introduction to calculus concepts (limits of sequences) |
| ELL students | Provide bilingual vocabulary lists (English/Spanish); allow diagrams and calculator use for communication |
| Students with limited technology access | Provide printed spreadsheet templates; allow hand-drawn graphs for all labs; pair with a partner for R labs |
| Students with test anxiety | Offer checkpoint quizzes as take-home or allow a second attempt |
| Students entering without S400 background | Provide a 1-page review of compound interest and basic R commands before Unit 1 |
